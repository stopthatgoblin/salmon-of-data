# Independent scientific and implementation review

Review dates: 23–24 September 2026. Role: adversarial review of the proposed satellite–probe inference, conducted separately from the implementation. The final numerical review uses the 82-row, 60-date screening dataset. This is a release assessment, not a claim that Sentinel turbidity retrieval is physically impossible.

## Decision at this stage

**No public whole-Lough NTU product is justified by this analysis.** The completed local feasibility screen also fails to show useful, robust spectral prediction relative to simple baselines. Missing aquatic atmospheric correction, unresolved probe quality/deployment metadata and sparse spatial reference coverage remain limitations of the available evidence. They must not be reported as a successful validation or as proof of fundamental satellite failure. Relative turbidity categories and anomalies require their own validation; they are not an automatic fallback.

The experiment should retain its research status even if a held-out regression score looks attractive. Two potentially useful lake stations cannot independently validate eight shoreline sectors and the open lake, and SCL class 6 is a scene classification, not certification of uncontaminated water-leaving reflectance.

## Critical radiometric finding

The legacy Earth Search `sentinel-2-l2a` COG collection has conflicting scale/offset provenance. The inspected example, `S2B_29UPA_20250911_0_L2A`, advertises `earthsearch:boa_offset_applied=true` while its STAC spectral assets specify scale `0.0001` and offset `-0.1`. The implementer found water DN values for which applying that offset again produces strongly negative reflectance. **Plausibility is not sufficient to decide which metadata to trust.**

This is supported by primary provider-repository evidence, rather than a speculative concern:

- The [Earth Search README](https://github.com/Element84/earth-search) instructs clients to apply nonzero asset scale/offset metadata.
- In the [provider's offset-design discussion](https://github.com/Element84/earth-search/discussions/26), maintainers describe legacy COG conversion applying the offset and flooring nonpositive adjusted pixels to DN 1. A later decision in the same discussion concerns a preview dataset retaining original values. Those statements cannot be collapsed into a universal rule for every collection and year.
- [Issue 66](https://github.com/Element84/earth-search/issues/66) supplies examples with inconsistent applied-offset metadata and identical scale/offset fields, including comparison against the original JP2 values. [Issue 71](https://github.com/Element84/earth-search/issues/71) reports 2025 scenes producing implausibly negative reflectance when the catalogue offset is applied. These are issue reports, not a provider-certified resolution of this project's files.

Required resolution: compare the exact processing baseline/product's COG and native JP2 or a demonstrably faithful Collection-1 asset over identical pixels. Preserve the source metadata and numerical comparison. Establish a per-asset conversion rule, reject unresolved assets, and flag any legacy DN-1 floor. Applying an offset twice breaks ratios; simply removing it globally risks breaking other assets. Negative or floored values must not be silently replaced by a small positive spectrum. A locally fitted intercept can hide a constant offset in some regressions, but it cannot restore clipped information or validate a published reflectance algorithm.

**Resolution for the screening experiment:** the implementation switched to the separate `sentinel-2-c1-l2a` collection and quarantined legacy arrays. The saved [radiometry audit](../../data/lough-neagh/derived/radiometry-audit.json) compares 1,470 band/station windows spanning 103 **identical SAFE product identifiers**. All 141,626 compared unclamped pixels have `C1 DN − legacy DN = 1000`; 907 non-nodata C1 pixels at or below DN 1000 and 963 legacy DN-1 pixels also expose the low-value floor issue. This is strong encoding evidence for using the C1 assets with their explicit scale `0.0001` and offset `−0.1` in this experiment. It is not independent field radiometric validation, and does not establish a global correction for unexamined legacy scenes. The final matcher rejects nonpositive or ≥1 values in its selected feature-band pixels instead of concealing them behind a positive median.

## Reference-data and spatial validity

1. **Deployment dates conflict with the observation history.** The captured authoritative station metadata records commissioning on 4 February 2026 for Rea's Wood, 2 February 2026 for Washing Bay and 16 January 2026 for Toome, while actual downloaded observations include 2025. Keep the earlier records; do not infer that they are erroneous. However, today's geometry, depth and instrument model do not prove that the station was in the same place with the same instrument throughout the earlier record. Flag this conflict, run a post-commission-date sensitivity analysis, and obtain deployment/move/instrument history before treating all rows as confirmed colocations.
2. **The published records are provisional and already selected by undisclosed source rules.** The station item explicitly warns that records are non-validated and revisable; the captured measurement schema has no observation-level quality field. However, independent inspection of that same schema confirms `viewDefinitionQuery` and `definitionQuery` are `(threshold_exceeded = 'N') AND (Published = 'Y') AND (reviewed = 'Y')`. These fields are absent from returned rows. It would be wrong to describe this as an unscreened feed: observations have already been selected upstream, and the threshold values/reasons are not exposed. The initial records' common upper envelope just below 40 NTU is a warning to investigate censoring, not a demonstrated environmental maximum or a proven exact threshold. The upstream `reviewed` condition also does not override the explicit non-validated disclaimer. The manufacturer's quoted instrument accuracy is not the uncertainty of an unattended, fouling-prone deployment. Calibration, cleaning and malfunction intervals are needed. A zero or extreme positive value is not automatically valid merely because the API returned it. Preserve raw rows and exclusion reasons; do not remove outliers using agreement with the satellite model. Performance conditional on this selected public feed cannot establish accuracy during excluded high-turbidity events.
3. **Toome is not a third offshore validation station.** Its captured coordinate is in the outlet outside the selected lake polygon and produced no candidate all-water SCL windows in the initial screen. Excluding it from a lake-pixel calibration is appropriate. Moving its coordinate offshore to force a match would manufacture colocation. River/outlet hydrodynamics also weaken assumptions that its water equals the adjacent lake's water.
4. **Geometric separation from shore is necessary but incomplete.** The initial polygon check places Rea's Wood and Washing Bay hundreds of metres inside the lake, but a cartographic polygon is not a per-scene waterline or an atmospheric adjacency model. Pixel footprints, islands, bathymetry, emergent vegetation, positional accuracy and changing water levels still matter. Two shore distances cannot support a meaningful continuous test of errors versus distance to shore.
5. **A half-metre probe samples a different volume.** Satellite spectral radiance integrates a wavelength-dependent surface layer over many square metres. Surface scums, vertical gradients, wave-related glint and advection can produce genuine disagreement without either sensor being broken. Phycocyanin RFU may help identify covariation, but it is not a cyanobacterial cell concentration, and turbidity is not suspended-sediment mass concentration.

## Matchup and quality controls

- Extract the same ground footprint in every band. A 3 × 3 20 m core must map to the corresponding 6 × 6 10 m footprint using geotransforms. Independently centring odd-sized arrays at each resolution can shift the support; array dimensions alone do not establish colocation. Retain each transform and station coordinate, and test this with stations near coarse-pixel boundaries.
- Cache validity must cover input coordinates, source assets/metadata, extraction settings and software version as well as byte hashes. Intact arrays from an earlier algorithm are still stale arrays. Checksums establish integrity, not scientific correctness.
- Nearest half-hour observations offer a defensible initial maximum separation of half the nominal interval, provided the actual timestamps bracket/cover that interval. Do not expand the tolerance to fill gaps. Retain signed separation, local before/after variability, gaps, duplicate conflicts and reference quality. A 15-minute rule follows sampling cadence; it is not a proof that the lake is stable for 15 minutes. Compare tighter matching and bracket variability where enough observations remain.
- A square 120 m cloud-neighbourhood rule is an exploratory screen. It needs a precise geometric definition, buffer sensitivity and scene review. It does not remove all thin haze, misclassified shadow, glint, aerosol adjacency or bloom contamination. A generic cloud percentage is not the fraction of usable Lough water.
- Spatial dispersion inside the extraction window should be retained as a diagnostic. It is not a calibrated prediction interval. Window-size selection based on the final test error would contaminate that test.

## Validation conditions

Keep complete acquisition dates on one side of each split, including overlapping granules or repeat acquisitions on that date. Outer date-held-out predictions are preferable to randomly splitting probe rows, but adjacent dates remain seasonally correlated; a forward-time/block assessment is also required. Separate station holdouts test transfer between these two stations only, not the whole lake.

Compare fixed simple spectral candidates with baselines learned from the training fold: global median, station median where the task permits known-station prediction, and an appropriately simple seasonal baseline. Otherwise a spectrum that identifies station or season can appear successful without tracking turbidity changes. No station-specific baseline should use the held-out station's target values. All fitting, transformations and model/window selection belong inside training data. Report all candidates; selecting the best cross-validation score and calling that selected score an untouched final-test result is optimistic.

Use out-of-fold MAE, RMSE, signed bias and R², retaining negative R²; inspect errors by date and station as well as pooled rows. State whether rows or independent dates are equally weighted. Percentage errors are unstable near zero. Date-block/bootstrap uncertainty around model comparisons should retain both stations together. Conformal or residual prediction intervals need separate calibration and empirical coverage assessment; pixel IQR, model spread and a metric confidence interval are not interchangeable with prediction uncertainty.

The current design does not yet establish residual behaviour by satellite viewing geometry, clouds, glint, bottom visibility or a complete range of bloom conditions. A missing covariate or a small subgroup must be reported as unassessed rather than as absence of bias. Reconstructing pre-2025 conditions requires an additional temporal/domain argument beyond modern held-out scores.

## Final implementation and numerical audit

Reviewed `build_matchups.py`, `extract_screening.py`, `audit_radiometry.py` and `model_screening.py`, plus the final [matchup ledger audit](../../data/lough-neagh/derived/matchup-audit.json), [predictions](../../data/lough-neagh/derived/exploratory-predictions.csv) and [validation diagnostics](../../data/lough-neagh/derived/exploratory-validation.json).

The final screened dataset contains **82 scene/station pairs on 60 distinct dates**: 47 at Rea's Wood and 35 at Washing Bay. It was selected from 1,035 candidate pairs for 345 scenes on 295 dates. The admitted target range is about 0.009–30.3 in the source's reported NTU. These are provisional source readings, not confirmed ground truth. Forty selected rows occur on/after the current station commissioning metadata; 42 occur before it. Both the full and post-commission-only analyses remain exploratory.

Independent checks performed:

- Verified complete-date separation for every actual leave-one-date, forward-time and station-plus-date fold, and also exercised the partition logic on a synthetic case with overlapping and non-overlapping station dates. For the station-plus-date folds, Rea's Wood has 21 training rows on 18 dates and 47 test rows on 42 dates; Washing Bay has 33 training rows on 30 dates and 35 test rows on 30 dates. No test station or test date occurs in the corresponding training data.
- Recomputed **all 24 model/split metric groups** from the saved prediction CSV using independent arithmetic, including date-balanced MAE/RMSE. Results agree with the report. Verified the report's input hash against the actual matchup CSV.
- Recomputed **328 leave-one-date predictions** for the global median, station median, red-only and red-plus-NIR candidates. Regression verification used an unstandardised design matrix and pseudoinverse independently of the implementation's standardised least-squares path. Predictions agree numerically.
- Confirmed that the common 60 m footprint is selected by geotransforms, that current geometry and source metadata are checked against cached values, and that missing/conflicting probe values and nonpositive selected-feature pixels are retained as exclusions. The initial null-value and independently-centred-window hazards have been addressed. Training-only centring/scaling avoids information leakage. Negative model predictions remain visible in the diagnostics rather than being clamped into plausible NTU.
- The remaining radiometry-audit hardening request is resolved: both C1 and legacy pixel-cache SHA-256 hashes are checked, and empty or unexpected/mixed unclamped differences stop the audit rather than producing an unconditional source recommendation. The implementation rerun passed; regression tests cover the empty, incorrect and mixed-difference cases. This closes the implementation request without changing the scientific no-go or the final 82-row dataset.

The central results, in units of the provisional feed's reported NTU, are:

| Holdout | Candidate | MAE | RMSE | R² |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Whole date | Training station median | 3.68 | 5.87 | 0.202 |
| Whole date | Red only | 4.58 | 6.65 | −0.024 |
| Whole date | Red plus NIR | 4.70 | 6.43 | 0.043 |
| Forward time blocks | Training station median | 5.13 | 7.76 | −0.067 |
| Forward time blocks | Red plus NIR | 5.57 | 8.17 | −0.184 |
| Station plus all its dates | Red plus NIR | 6.35 | 8.35 | −0.615 |

All five spectral candidates have worse whole-date MAE and RMSE than the station-median baseline. Every forward-time candidate has negative R². Individual spectral candidates slightly improve a particular forward metric, but do not show robust improvement across error measures or transfer tests. The best whole-date spectral R² is only 0.043, from red plus NIR; that is not evidence for useful lake-wide estimates.

Pooled bias is especially misleading here: the red-only model's whole-date average bias is approximately +0.01 NTU, but its site biases are **+3.07 at Rea's Wood and −4.10 at Washing Bay**. The two errors cancel. Among the 11 selected target values ≥10 NTU, red-only predictions underpredict by an average 14.5 NTU and red-plus-NIR by 13.5 NTU. These diagnostic bins are not ecological or public-health thresholds. Winter error is also larger, but its subgroup is small. Such patterns preclude interpreting the near-zero pooled bias as accuracy.

The post-commission-only sensitivity does not rescue the result: on 40 rows over 26 dates, red-plus-NIR MAE is 4.93, RMSE 6.94 and R² −0.41, against station-median MAE 3.68 and RMSE 5.62. Tightening the red-window variation rule to 0.1 leaves 70 rows on 51 dates and still gives red-plus-NIR R² approximately −0.01. The 300 m shoreline sensitivity retains only Washing Bay; it changes the location and target distribution and therefore cannot isolate an adjacency effect.

Residual correlations against phycocyanin RFU, tile cloud percentage, sun elevation and incidence angle have been added as descriptive diagnostics. They neither establish a causal error mechanism nor rule out confounding; tile cloud percentage is not a local cloud/glint metric. No calibrated prediction intervals, relative-turbidity categories, anomaly model, offshore transfer, or historical reconstruction were validated. The bootstrap intervals describe resampling of conditional prediction errors by date, with fixed fitted predictions; they do not include model-fitting/model-selection instability or systematic reference/atmospheric error.

**Final review judgement:** the calculations support rejection of the **tested Sen2Cor L2A plus selected public-probe modelling route for a public NTU tracker**. The available data also fail the reference-quality and spatial-transfer requirements independently of those poor scores. They do **not** demonstrate that aquatic-corrected Sentinel retrieval, an independently assessed Copernicus product, or future locally calibrated retrieval with adequate field observations must fail. No public map, broad turbidity category, or claimed historical reconstruction should be generated from these fits.

## Evidence required to reopen publication

Resolve radiometry and coordinate/deployment history; obtain quality-controlled reference measurements and maintenance flags; compare a water-specific L1C atmospheric-correction branch with any retained L2A comparator; independently assess the Copernicus lake-water product on its correct temporal support; and obtain spatial transects spanning the intended sectors, offshore water and relevant bloom/sediment conditions. A prospective held-out campaign with turbidity plus above-water radiometry and relevant water constituents would distinguish atmospheric, reference and retrieval errors.

Until then, a no-go for publication is the scientifically appropriate outcome. It is a statement of **not yet demonstrated**, with several concrete implementation hazards, rather than an assertion that no defensible Sentinel turbidity product can ever exist for Lough Neagh.
