# Lough Neagh satellite turbidity: literature and methodological review

Research date: 23 September 2026. This document is a literature review and proposed analysis protocol, **not evidence that a Lough Neagh model has passed validation**. Source access was by publisher, institutional repository, instrument guidance, or official product documentation. Search-engine publication/crawl ages were not treated as publication dates. Where full text was unavailable, conclusions below are limited accordingly.

## Scientific judgement

**Sentinel-2 is a credible instrument for investigating spatial turbidity in Lough Neagh, but the literature does not establish that an arbitrary red/NIR formula produces accurate local NTU.** Whether it can support this public product must be decided with independent local matchups and an explicit application domain. A data-access failure or missing reference metadata means *not yet demonstrated*, not *physically impossible*. A colour image, agreement between two satellite products, and a high training correlation are three different things; none substitutes for a local held-out comparison with probes.

The most defensible initial experiment is modest: compare a few aquatic atmospheric corrections and published red/red-edge/NIR retrievals, simple local regressions, and the existing Copernicus product on the **same acceptable overpasses**. Keep a seasonal or station-median baseline. Reserve complete overpasses and then stations/temporal blocks for evaluation. Stop before public NTU mapping if reference observations, station representativeness, or held-out performance are inadequate. Relative optical anomalies are a separate hypothesis to validate, not an automatic escape route after NTU failure.

## Evidence: established algorithms and comparison studies

### Red/NIR semi-empirical retrieval

[Dogliotti et al. (2015), *A single algorithm to retrieve turbidity from remotely-sensed data in all coastal and estuarine waters*](https://tele.iafe.uba.ar/filesMarine/Papers/2015_Dogliotti_etal_RSE.pdf), *Remote Sensing of Environment* 156, 157–168, relates turbidity to water-leaving reflectance as `T = A × ρw / (1 − ρw/C)`, with wavelength-dependent coefficients. It combines red sensitivity at low turbidity with NIR sensitivity at higher turbidity. The published MODIS implementation switches using red-reflectance bounds 0.05 and 0.07 and reports FNU. Its calibration and evaluation concern coastal/estuarine sediment-dominated waters. These coefficients and switch points are **not locally established thresholds for Lough Neagh**, and broad Sentinel-2 B8 is not equivalent to the original 859 nm channel. This is a sensible benchmark after spectral-response and reflectance-convention checks, not a turnkey NTU measurement. Near the denominator singularity retrieval becomes unstable; masking and saturation reporting are essential.

The foundational Nechad family should be treated carefully: suspended particulate matter (mass concentration) and turbidity (nephelometric response) are different outputs even when an identical equation structure is used. Record the exact coefficient table, sensor spectral response, output quantity, units and implementation version. Do not select coefficients from a differently named product because its numerical scale looks plausible.

### Canadian inland-water evidence

[*Using remote sensing to assess how intensive agriculture impacts the turbidity of a fluvial lake floodplain* (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jglr.2023.102240), *Journal of Great Lakes Research* 49(6), 102240, is a directly relevant Sentinel-2 study of Lake Saint-Pierre, Quebec. It uses red and NIR reflectances to map floodplain turbidity in the 2019 and 2020 spring floods. It establishes Canadian precedent for a simple, physically motivated, locally fitted approach in shallow sediment-rich water. Its land-use results are not a test of portability to a cyanobacteria-rich lake.

The related [UQTR thesis manuscript](https://depot-e.uqtr.ca/id/eprint/10638/1/eprint10638.pdf) documents eight calibration images in 2019, Sen2Cor L2A imagery, probe transects, and 3 × 3 reflectance averaging. It reports adjusted R² 0.942 and RMSE 18.27 FNU for the fitted combined red/NIR model. **These are manuscript fit statistics; this review did not establish independent date-held-out performance from that manuscript and does not describe them as out-of-sample accuracy.** Thousands of samples along eight dates are not thousands of independent atmospheric conditions. The final journal article may differ from the thesis, so equations should be checked against the final version before implementing them.

[*Assessing the effects of land use on water turbidity in a fluvial lake floodplain using Sentinel-2 imagery* (2025)](https://doi.org/10.2166/wqrj.2025.009), *Water Quality Research Journal*, provides further Lake Saint-Pierre evidence. The publisher describes a local red-band model for flooded forest openings with adjusted R² 0.88 and RMSE 10.91 FNU. Vegetation/open-water masking and the domain-specific floodplain calibration matter. Those figures do not establish its performance in Lough Neagh, across seasons, or under independent-overpass holdouts.

[*Genetic Algorithm for Atmospheric Correction (GAAC) of water bodies impacted by adjacency effects*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0034425724005340) evaluates a simultaneous atmosphere, glint, adjacency, reflectance and inherent-optical-property method using in-situ reflectance from approximately 100 Canadian lakes. It reports improvements over ACOLITE and iCOR+SIMEC. This is evidence that Canadian lake adjacency is a substantive retrieval issue, **not turbidity validation**. Availability, maintainability and independent local benefit would need checking before adopting a new processor operationally.

[*Assessing the Impact of T-Mart Adjacency Effect Correction on Turbidity Retrieval from Landsat 8/9 and Sentinel-2 Imagery (Case Study: St. Lawrence River, Canada)*](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/18/1/127), *Remote Sensing* 18(1), 127, studies four 2024 field campaigns with 16,074 transect observations. It compares atmospheric/adjacency preprocessing with LightGBM. The reported workflow selects spatial kernels using the test-set RMSE; that selection makes that set partly a tuning set. This is a reason to use a separate outer holdout here. Four field campaigns are also a weak basis for claims about general future-overpass performance, however large the row count. The study motivates testing adjacency correction; it does not justify copying a complex learner.

### Other optically complex lakes

[Tavares et al. (2025)](https://horizon.documentation.ird.fr/exl-doc/pleins_textes/2025-09/010094848.pdf), *A Framework to Retrieve Water Quality Parameters in Small, Optically Diverse Freshwater Ecosystems Using Sentinel-2 MSI Imagery*, *Remote Sensing* 17, 2729, combines glint-corrected reflectance and optical-water-type approaches across 108 French lakes. Turbidity comparisons report RMSE 9.7 NTU, correlation 0.87 and median absolute percentage error 47%. The study uses 3 × 3 spatial samples and ±5-day matchups to expand its reference dataset. Longer NIR bands remain sensitive to adjacency. **Do not transplant the ±5-day tolerance into Lough Neagh:** the availability of half-hour probes allows much stricter matching, and wind, runoff and surface blooms can change quickly. The study demonstrates potential and substantial remaining error, not universal accuracy.

[Warren, Simis and Selmes (2021)](https://plymsea.ac.uk/id/eprint/9328/1/1-s2.0-S0034425721003710-main.pdf), *Complementary water quality observations from high and medium resolution Sentinel sensors by aligning chlorophyll-a and turbidity algorithms*, *RSE* 265, 112651, aligns Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-3 retrievals over 24 lakes. It finds useful sensor consistency but persistent near-land disagreement. **This is sensor-to-sensor tuning, not a local probe validation.** Its small intersensor differences must not be presented as Lough Neagh NTU accuracy. The paper also makes the tuning conditional on atmospheric correction and radiometric processing: changing either may require retuning. Its evidence supports keeping processing baselines and atmospheric-correction versions in provenance.

## Atmospheric correction and observation quality

[Warren et al. (2019)](https://plymsea.ac.uk/id/eprint/8168/1/1-s2.0-S0034425719301099-main%20%281%29.pdf), *Assessment of atmospheric correction algorithms for the Sentinel-2A MultiSpectral Imager over coastal and inland waters*, *RSE* 225, 267–289, compares processors against field radiometry across optically diverse waters. [Pahlevan et al. (2021), ACIX-Aqua](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210011543/downloads/ACIX-Aqua%20A%20global%20assessment%20of%20atmospheric%20correction%20methods%20for%20Landsat-8.pdf), *RSE* 258, 112366, extends that evidence to eight processors and more than 1,000 radiometric matchups. The performance varies with water type and validation dataset. Neither establishes a universal best processor. Consequently, official L2A surface reflectance should be a comparator, not automatically the authoritative aquatic input. A local regression on L2A could absorb scene-specific atmospheric error and then fail elsewhere or in another season.

[Vanhellemont (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2019.03.010), *Adaptation of the dark spectrum fitting atmospheric correction for aquatic applications of the Landsat and Sentinel-2 archives*, offers an operational candidate: ACOLITE dark spectrum fitting estimates atmospheric path reflectance using multiple dark targets and supports tiled scene processing and optional glint correction. Start from L1C for this branch; do not atmospherically correct an already corrected L2A product again. Pin software/settings and retain intermediate reflectance. [Harmel et al. (2018), SWIR sunglint correction for Sentinel-2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0034425717304856), shows why sunglint needs explicit treatment and why assuming zero NIR water signal is unsafe in turbid water. A broad reflective patch should never be presumed to be turbidity until glint has been examined.

Proposed quality treatment, to be validated locally:

| Issue | Consequence and defensible action |
|---|---|
| Clouds / thin cirrus / shadows | Use pixel masks and neighbourhood checks, not tile cloud percentage alone. Retain mask reason and visible clear-water fraction. Inspect known false positives and negatives. No filled values under clouds. |
| Cloud edges / haze | Evaluate distance-to-cloud and buffer sensitivity. Choose production rules from reviewed examples and held-out errors; a chosen operational buffer is not an established physical constant. |
| Shoreline mixing | Intersect an authoritative shoreline with image-based water evidence and raster pixel footprints. Keep an inward buffer and explicit distance to shore. A 10 m centre offshore does not prove a whole pixel is water. |
| Atmospheric adjacency | A geometric shoreline buffer addresses mixing, but scattered light can extend farther. Examine residuals against shore distance, vegetation and aerosol conditions. Consider iCOR/SIMEC or another adjacency treatment only if it improves independent validation. |
| Bottom reflection / vegetation | Depth alone does not define optical shallowness. Combine available bathymetry with attenuation/clarity evidence and reject bottom-visible/submerged-vegetation pixels. Do not impose a universal depth cutoff without local evidence. |
| Cyanobacterial surface accumulations | Mask or create a separately validated observation class. Probe water at 0.5 m and floating surface scum can be very different samples. Do not call scum reflectance suspended sediment. |
| Negative / saturated reflectance | Record failures; do not clamp spectra into the model's accepted range and thereby create plausible estimates. |
| Platform / baseline differences | Apply product scale/offset metadata correctly; keep platform and baseline in every row. Check residuals by S2A/B/C. Do not upsample red-edge bands and claim new 10 m information. |

Official [Sentinel-2 processing documentation](https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s2-processing) describes SCL and evolving cloud/shadow processing, including baseline-dependent dilation. Therefore masks need version provenance and spot checks through the archive. A stable numeric SCL class across years is not proof of an identical algorithm. Cloud-obscured periods also cause sampling bias: a clear-sky time series is not an unbiased record of all weather or all sediment events.

## Lough Neagh-specific findings

[Reid et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envint.2024.108934), *Unprecedented Harmful algal bloom in the UK and Ireland’s largest lake…*, *Environment International* 190, 108934, uses Sentinel-2 in its study of the 2023 bloom. The [institutional full text](https://pure.qub.ac.uk/files/606829855/1-s2.0-S0160412024005208-main.pdf) explains that cloud/unavailability left its nearest image two weeks after sampling; it used NDVI and 500 m surroundings as general bloom context. This is **not matched turbidity validation**. It demonstrates both relevant biological optical complexity and how sparse clear imagery can be. The paper's field toxin/bacteria results cannot be inferred from a turbidity raster.

[*Sand dredging is associated with benthic habitat alteration and chronic turbidity with profound ecological consequences likely for the UK and Ireland’s largest freshwater lake* (2026)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301479726010339) uses Sentinel-2/Landsat imagery to identify extraction-associated plumes and vessel tracks, combined with bathymetry and hydrodynamic work. It supports the existence of spatially resolved sediment patterns, but the inspected abstract does not establish a locally validated satellite NTU retrieval. Interpretation of causal mechanisms is separate from estimation of turbidity.

The [University of Stirling HABIT project description](https://www.stir.ac.uk/about/scotlands-international-environment-centre/environmental-data-services/water/satellite-freshwater-quality/) confirms existing satellite monitoring work on Lough Neagh. It is a relevant potential collaborator/processing source, not a peer-reviewed validation dataset. No independently held-out Lough Neagh Sentinel-2-versus-DAERA NTU result was identified in this search. This is a search finding rather than proof that none exists.

## Existing processed products: use as benchmarks first

The official [CLMS 100 m LWQ v2 catalogue](https://land.copernicus.eu/en/products/water-bodies/lake-water-quality-v2-0-100m) describes Sentinel-2, 2024–present, 10-day products, marked validated, available through CDSE. It is a serious candidate, but a catalogue validation label does not establish accuracy at these stations. A 10-day synthesis must be matched to its actual temporal support and labelled as a period; it must not be presented as one instantaneous overpass. Check pixel availability and flags over Lough Neagh rather than assuming catalogue coverage proves usable data.

The [v2 release note](https://land.copernicus.eu/en/news/lake-water-quality-global-products-version-2-release) states that turbidity is now calculated directly from reflectance, replacing earlier derivation through suspended particulate matter, and adds quality flags. It advertises per-pixel uncertainty for **chlorophyll-a and TSM in the 300 m product**, not a blanket per-pixel turbidity uncertainty at 100 m. Do not copy another variable's uncertainty onto turbidity or splice v1 and v2 without a documented overlap analysis. Sentinel-3 at 300 m can complement lake-wide/large-sector analysis but cannot honestly replace fine shoreline detail.

The [EOCIS Lake Catchment guide v1.2](https://eocis.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/UK_EOCIS_LakeCatchment_Quick-Start-Guide-V1.2.pdf) explicitly demonstrates Lough Neagh 100 m aquatic reflectance, turbidity and chlorophyll for individual available Sentinel-2 observations in 2016–2023, without temporal aggregation. [CEDA catalogue](https://catalogue.ceda.ac.uk/uuid/6e329e32570d4d4f818b8f8aa18e7a85/) is the stated access point. However, this is a demonstration dataset supplied without strict filtering and requiring expert use. The guide warns that 2023 floating cyanobacteria produced erroneous mixed-column chlorophyll estimates. Its turbidity unit description is ambiguous (“Normalised Turbidity Units”); inspect NetCDF metadata and resolve the instrument/algorithm convention before comparing to DAERA NTU. This is an excellent historical candidate, not an automatically publication-ready archive. Respect EOCIS attribution conditions.

## Reference measurements and units

[DAERA's official FAQ](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/articles/lough-neagh-water-quality-dashboard-faq) describes measurements approximately 50 cm below the surface, half-hour buoy reporting with a seven-day lag, and downloadable turbidity labelled NTU. It warns of sensor errors, including biofouling and blockage. Exact periods must come from downloaded observations, not catalogue publication dates. Satellite retrievals sample a wavelength-dependent optical surface layer and a pixel area, so even a correctly matched probe is not an error-free observation of exactly the same volume.

[USGS turbidity field guidance](https://pubs.usgs.gov/twri/twri9a6/twri9a67/twri9a_Section6.7_v2.1.pdf) distinguishes broadband NTU from infrared FNU conventions and notes that different instruments may agree on calibration standards but disagree on environmental samples. It also notes that manufacturer labels may use NTU generically. Thus **never apply a numerical FNU-to-NTU conversion of 1 merely because values look comparable**. Obtain the actual DAERA instrument model, light source/scattering geometry, firmware/calibration and cleaning records. Retain the publisher's original unit label. A model calibrated to DAERA values may be described as an estimate on that instrument's reported NTU scale, with the method explained; a published algorithm benchmark keeps its own output units.

## Proposed reproducible matchup protocol

The following are project recommendations, not retrospectively chosen rules claimed to be universal standards.

1. **Time:** Store exact UTC acquisition and probe times and resolve source timezone explicitly. Start with the closest valid half-hour observation, allowing at most 15 minutes when cadence is genuinely regular; additionally retain bracketing observations and variability over the ±1-hour neighbourhood. This 15-minute starting choice follows the source cadence, not a physical constancy claim. If observations are missing, mark unmatched rather than widen silently. Compare sensitivity using strict nearest, bracketing interpolation only when both valid and stable, and justified ±30/60-minute summaries. Any final permitted window must follow measured temporal variability, probe precision and matchup uncertainty; fit these rules using development data only.
2. **Spatial support:** Begin with a 3 × 3 native-grid neighbourhood using the common effective resolution required by the predictor bands; retain median, IQR/MAD, valid count and pixel geometry. Test a central pixel and larger windows within development folds. Do not treat the nine pixels as nine independent NTU observations. Do not move a shoreline station hundreds of metres offshore to obtain an attractive match: that becomes a different water mass unless transects establish representativeness. Exclude stations with no defensible sampling footprint and report them.
3. **Quality:** Every candidate receives accepted/rejected status and explicit reasons. Include coordinate precision, depth, provisional flag, maintenance flags, nearest time difference, temporal variability, shore/cloud distances, spectral spread, glint estimate, bloom diagnostic, view/sun geometry and processing baseline. Keep masks separate from measured values. Daily probe means are inappropriate labels for instantaneous images unless independently justified.
4. **One analytical unit:** One station × overpass × defined spatial support is the basic row. Keep all granules from the same sensing event in the same split; deduplicate overlaps. Store every retained predictor and original target with source IDs and a file checksum. Rejected rows are needed to show selection bias.

[Salama et al. (2022), fixed-station Sentinel validation](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/remote-sensing/articles/10.3389/frsen.2021.808287/full), discusses conventional ±1-hour matching and the extra error caused by spatial, depth and temporal mismatch. Its use of continuous observations to assess representativeness supports diagnosing dynamics rather than adopting arbitrary multi-day tolerances. Its specific estuarine statistical rescaling procedure should not be copied to improve apparent accuracy on the held-out observations. [IOCCG protocols](https://ioccg.org/what-we-do/ioccg-publications/ioccg-protocols/) provide field-radiometry practice and link dedicated inland-water guidance; simultaneous water-leaving radiometry would help separate atmospheric failure from turbidity-model failure.

## Model comparison, validation and uncertainty

Predeclare a compact candidate set: published red and red-edge Nechad implementations, a spectrally appropriate red/NIR switching benchmark, red-only local linear or log-response regression, one or two motivated band-ratio models, and a low-dimensional regularized regression if independent data volume permits. Add CLMS/EOCIS with their own units/support. Include a training-only median or season/station climatology baseline. Empirical indices such as NDTI are unitless and are not NTU before calibration. Sentinel-1 radar may provide surface-condition context but should not be used to fabricate optical turbidity through clouds.

Use outer splits by whole overpass to estimate future-scene performance. In addition, assess forward-in-time blocks and leave-station-out performance where feasible. Keep preprocessing choices, window selection, coefficients, feature scaling, hyperparameters and uncertainty fitting inside the training side of each outer split. Prefer nested tuning or a very small fixed candidate set to repeated inspection of a single test set. There is no universal acceptable minimum scene count: report the actual count, season/range coverage, unstable confidence intervals and learning curves. A large probe table cannot repair a small independent scene count. This design follows the general dependence warning in [Roberts et al. (2017), *Cross-validation strategies for data with temporal, spatial, hierarchical, or phylogenetic structure*](https://www.wsl.ch/lud/biodiversity_events/papers/Roberts_et_al-2017-Ecography.pdf).

Report pooled **out-of-fold** MAE, RMSE, signed bias, R² (allowing negative values), and errors by overpass and station. State whether percentage metrics are mean or median; suppress percentages near zero or detection limits and show absolute errors. Add residuals against target level, month, station, shore distance, cloud/glint conditions, bloom diagnostics, sensor and geometry. [Seegers et al. (2018)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70265020), *Performance metrics for the assessment of satellite data products: An ocean color case study*, supports a multi-metric, use-dependent assessment and cautions against relying on squared-error metrics alone for skewed data.

Distinguish four quantities: variation **within a sector**, probe/matchup uncertainty, uncertainty in a median statistic, and prediction error for an individual pixel. They are not interchangeable. Estimate metric confidence intervals by resampling complete overpasses, not individual rows. Prediction intervals should be calibrated using held-out overpasses (or carefully justified grouped conformal/residual methods) and their empirical coverage and width tested on independent data. Sparse groups or distribution shifts weaken guarantees and must be disclosed. An ensemble's spread or IQR of lake pixels is not a validated prediction interval.

## Publication decision and consequences

The product may show NTU only after independently assessed errors are materially smaller than the differences it invites readers to interpret, intervals have credible coverage, calibration spans the displayed range and conditions, and validation supports the mapped locations. A station-specific model does not establish whole-lake accuracy. Any numerical launch criterion is a project decision to be justified before final-test inspection; the literature does not supply a universal “R² > X means safe” rule.

If absolute retrieval fails but ranks/anomalies pass their own independent tests across seasons and optical conditions, display explicitly labelled relative optical/turbidity indicators with uncertainty. Class boundaries must derive from a declared purpose or validated statistical construction; “low/medium/high” must not imply established ecological or safety thresholds. If cloud/adjacency/biology confounding prevents reliable spatial ranking, publish the research findings and actual in-situ measurements only.

Required additions if the present data cannot establish feasibility: verified probe coordinates and method/QA metadata; valid observations covering multiple independent clear scenes and seasons; shore-to-offshore transects across the intended sectors; coincident above-water radiometry; turbidity, chlorophyll/phycocyanin, suspended solids and clarity/depth observations sufficient to diagnose composition and bottom effects; and maintenance/fouling histories. These additions distinguish a fixable validation gap from failure of the satellite approach itself.

Historical reconstruction must follow the successful model's validated domain. Do not infer that 2025–2026 calibration validates pre-bloom-regime years, or that modern S2C performance validates older baselines. Retain an explicit out-of-domain flag and leave unsupported cells missing. Changes between sector dates should use comparable support or disclose changing coverage; clear-area medians must not be labelled whole-sector medians when missingness could be spatially biased. Seasonal percentiles need a sufficiently representative archive and must be described as percentiles of usable satellite observations, not all lake conditions.
